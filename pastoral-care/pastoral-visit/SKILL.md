# Pastoral Visit Prep

Ten minutes of preparation before you walk in.

> Requires: pastor-foundation skill
> **Tier 2.** This gets you ready. It does not tell you what to say.

---

## Confidentiality First

**Do not give a name.** Use a role or initials. This skill works entirely from the
situation.

Nothing from this skill is written to disk unless you explicitly ask. Nothing about
the person is ever searched for online. If you paste in details that identify someone,
they will be used for this conversation and will not appear in any generated file.

If a name appears in something you ask to be saved, it will be replaced with initials.

---

## What You Need to Provide

**Required:**
- The situation type (see the list below)
- Where the visit happens: hospital, home, office, phone, coffee shop

**Helpful:**
- How well you know them
- How long the situation has been going on
- Whether they asked for the visit or you initiated
- What you are worried about going into it
- Anything you already tried that did not land

**Minimum to start:** the situation type. One line is enough.

### Situation types

Hospital (acute) · Hospital (terminal) · Home visit after a loss · Anticipatory grief ·
Marriage crisis · Addiction or relapse · Mental health crisis · Financial crisis ·
Church conflict · Confession or moral failure · Doubt or deconstruction · Job loss ·
Estrangement · Chronic illness or caregiving · Prodigal child · New diagnosis ·
Miscarriage or infertility · Elder care decisions

If it is not on the list, describe it. The list is not a gate.

---

## Step 1: What You Are Walking Into

A short read of the situation. What is probably happening under the surface, what the
person most likely needs from you, and what they almost certainly do not need.

Name the gap between what will be asked for and what is actually needed, when there is
one. People ask for answers when they want company.

---

## Step 2: What to Expect in the Room

Practical and specific:

- Likely emotional state, and how it may change mid-conversation
- Who else may be present, and how that changes what can be said
- Physical realities: hospital equipment, a chair positioned badly, interruptions,
  time limits, whether to sit or stand
- How long this should probably last. Most visits go too long.
- Signs it is time to leave

---

## Step 3: Questions to Have Ready

6-10 questions, ordered from safest to deepest, so you can move at their pace instead
of yours.

For each: what it opens up, and when not to use it.

Include at least two questions that give permission to say the thing they think they
are not allowed to say. Those are usually the ones that matter.

---

## Step 4: What Not to Say

Specific to this situation, not generic. The well-meant things that wound:

- The clichés this particular situation attracts
- The scripture that gets weaponized here and why it lands wrong
- The reassurance that is not yours to give
- The advice reflex, and what to do instead of it

Be blunt. This section exists to prevent real harm.

---

## Step 5: Scripture and Prayer

2-4 passages in `BIBLE_TRANSLATION` worth having ready, with one line on what each does
and when it would be wrong.

Include at least one lament passage. Grief does not need to be resolved to be biblical.

For prayer: what to pray toward, and whether to pray aloud at all. Sometimes the answer
is no. Say so when it is.

Ask before praying. Always.

---

## Step 6: Referral Check

Flag clearly when this situation needs more than a pastor:

- Suicidal ideation, self-harm, or a plan
- Abuse of any kind, including mandatory reporting obligations
- Active addiction requiring medical detox
- Psychosis or a mental health emergency
- Legal exposure for the person or the church

When any of these are present, say so first, before anything else in the output. Name
what the referral is and what your role becomes once it is made. Pastoral presence
continues; pastoral treatment does not begin.

---

## Step 7: Follow-Up

- What to do in the next 24 hours
- What to do in the next week
- What to put on the calendar so it does not get lost
- What to hand off, and to whom
- What to write down for yourself, and what not to write down anywhere

Include one line on caring for yourself after a heavy visit. Pastors absorb these and
then preach three days later.

---

## Output Format

Markdown to `output/`, filename by situation type and date, never by name:

```
output/Visit-Prep-hospital-terminal-2026-08-02.md
```

No PDF. This is a private working document, not something to print and carry.

If the visit is imminent, lead with a compressed version: the three things to remember,
the three things not to say. Then the full prep below it.

---

## Quality Bar

**Done looks like:** you walk in less anxious, more present, and better able to listen
than if you had gone in cold.

**Failure modes:**
- Scripting the conversation. You are preparing a person, not delivering a program.
- Generic advice that would fit any situation
- Missing a referral trigger. This is the most serious failure here.
- Recommending scripture that explains suffering to someone still inside it
- Writing a name to disk

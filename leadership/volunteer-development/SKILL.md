# Volunteer Development

The bottleneck is almost never willingness. It is clarity.

> Requires: pastor-foundation skill
> **Tier 3.** Drafts fully.

---

## What You Need to Provide

**Required:** the role you need filled, or the problem you are having.

Valid inputs: "I need four more kids volunteers." "My worship team keeps quitting."
"Nobody signs up for anything." "I have a volunteer I need to move out of a role."

**Helpful:**
- How many you need and by when
- What the role actually requires: hours, skills, background check, training
- What you have already tried
- Who is currently doing it and how they are holding up
- Whether this is a recruiting problem or a retention problem

**Minimum to start:** one sentence about the role or the problem.

Right-size to `ATTENDANCE`. A church of 90 cannot run a team of 20. If the ask exceeds
what the church can plausibly supply, say so and propose the smaller version that
actually works.

---

## Step 1: Diagnose Before Prescribing

Recruiting and retention problems look identical from the outside and need opposite
solutions.

Ask, or infer from what was provided:

| Symptom | Usually means |
|---|---|
| Nobody signs up | The ask is vague, too big, or open-ended |
| People sign up and quit | Onboarding, expectations, or the role itself is broken |
| The same eight people do everything | No pipeline, and the eight are a bottleneck nobody wants to disturb |
| Volunteers show up but disengage | No development, no thanks, no sense of why it matters |
| The role has been empty for months | The role may not need to exist |

That last one is real. Ask whether the ministry should continue before recruiting for
it. A church of 90 running six ministries badly would be better running three well.

**Output:** one paragraph naming the actual problem. Say it directly if the stated
problem is not the real one.

---

## Step 2: Role Clarity

Nobody volunteers for ambiguity. Define the role in a form you could hand to someone:

- **Title**, in plain language
- **What you will actually do**, concretely
- **Time cost**, honestly. Include prep, not just the Sunday hours. Understating this
  is the single most common cause of burnout.
- **Commitment length**, with an end date. "Indefinitely" is why people say no.
- **Who you report to**, by name
- **What is provided**: training, materials, support
- **What is required**: background check, membership, skills, a faith commitment
- **Why it matters**, in one sentence that is true

The end date is the highest-leverage item here. A six-month commitment with a real
off-ramp gets far more yeses than an open-ended one, and most people renew.

---

## Step 3: Recruiting

**The ask itself.** Personal asks work; announcements mostly do not. Provide:
- What to say in a one-on-one ask, as talking points rather than a script
- A short version for a text or email
- A platform announcement, only if it is genuinely a volume play

**Who to ask.** Help identify candidates: people already adjacent, people who have
expressed interest, people finishing another commitment, people newer than you think.

**Handling no.** How to receive it without damaging the relationship, and how to leave
the door open. Someone who says no well is often a yes in six months.

Never guilt. Never imply that declining is a spiritual failure. It poisons the well and
it is not true.

---

## Step 4: Onboarding

The first 90 days determine whether someone stays.

- Before the first shift: what they need to know, have, and have signed
- The first shift: who meets them, who they shadow, what "done" looks like
- The first month: a check-in with a real date
- The 90-day conversation: is this working, honestly, for both sides

Include the safety and policy items that are not optional: background checks, child
protection policy, two-adult rules, mandatory reporting. Do not treat these as
paperwork. Name them as protection for children and for the volunteer.

---

## Step 5: Development and Retention

- **Thanks** that is specific and not seasonal. "Thanks for serving" does nothing.
- **Growth**: what the next step looks like for someone who is good at this
- **Rest**: how someone takes a break without quitting, and permission to do it
- **The exit**: how someone leaves well, and how you find out before they are gone

Include how to move someone out of a role they are not right for. It is the hardest
conversation in volunteer ministry and most pastors avoid it until it becomes a crisis.
Give the structure: what to say, what not to say, what to offer instead.

## Quality Bar

**Done looks like:** you can make the ask this week, and the person saying yes knows
exactly what they said yes to.

**Failure modes:**
- Recruiting for a role that should be eliminated
- Understating the time cost
- Open-ended commitments with no end date
- Guilt in the ask
- Skipping the safety requirements
- A plan that needs staff capacity the church does not have

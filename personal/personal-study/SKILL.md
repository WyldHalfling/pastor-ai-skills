# Personal Study

Read for yourself. Not for Sunday.

> Requires: pastor-foundation skill
> **Tier 1.** Research and questions only. Nothing here is drafted for you to say.

---

## Why This Exists Separately

Pastors lose the ability to read scripture without hunting for sermon material. Every
passage becomes a resource. That is an occupational hazard, and it starves the person
doing the feeding.

This skill deliberately produces nothing usable on Sunday. No big ideas, no outlines,
no illustrations, no application points aimed at a congregation. If you find yourself
wanting to route the output into `sermon-research`, that is exactly the instinct this
skill exists to interrupt.

If you actually need sermon prep, use `sermon-research`. That is a different tool for a
different job, and there is no shame in needing that one instead.

---

## What You Need to Provide

**Required:** a passage, a question, or a state you are in.

That is genuinely it. "I'm reading Mark 4," "Why does Ecclesiastes get to say that,"
"I'm exhausted and I don't know where to read" are all valid inputs.

**Optional:**
- How much time you have
- Whether you want to sit in one passage or range
- Whether this connects to seminary coursework

---

## Step 1: Situate the Text

Brief. Enough to read honestly, not a lecture.

- Where this sits in the book and why that matters
- Genre, and what it demands of a reader
- The historical situation, in a few sentences

If you are ranging rather than studying one passage, skip this and go to Step 3.

---

## Step 2: Observations

Things actually in the text that are easy to read past. Repetitions, structure, a word
that carries weight, a shift in who is speaking, something the text refuses to explain.

Note where translations differ meaningfully, and what is behind the difference.

Do not tell me what it means for my life. Tell me what is there.

---

## Step 3: Questions to Sit With

5-8 questions. Not comprehension questions. The kind that stay with you through the
afternoon.

At least two should be uncomfortable, and at least one should be aimed at the pastor
rather than the reader in general. The occupational hazards are real: performing
faith, resenting the congregation, hiding behind competence, preaching what you are
not currently living.

No answers provided. That is the point.

---

## Step 4: Where This Is Hard

Name honestly what is difficult about the passage: the ethical problem, the historical
issue, the part that does not resolve, the place where the standard reading feels like
it is smoothing something over.

Do not resolve it. Do not offer the three views and pick one. Let it be hard.

---

## Step 5: Prayer Direction

One paragraph, in the register of the passage. Lament if the text laments. Praise if it
praises. Silence if that is the honest response.

Not a written prayer to recite. A direction to pray in.

---

## Optional: Seminary Bridge

If this connects to M.Div. coursework, add a short section noting the academic angle:
what a paper would need to engage, where the scholarly conversation sits, what sources
would matter.

Keep it clearly separated from the devotional material. The whole point is that these
are different modes of reading, and collapsing them is how a pastor stops being fed.

---

## Output Format

Markdown to `output/`, named by passage and date:

```
output/Study-Mark-4-2026-08-02.md
```

No PDF. This is not a document for anyone else.

Short by default. A study that takes longer to read than to pray through has failed.

---

## Quality Bar

**Done looks like:** you read the passage differently than you would have alone, and
you did not once think about Sunday.

**Failure modes:**
- Producing anything preachable. If a section could be lifted into a sermon, cut it.
- Answering the questions
- Resolving the hard parts
- Turning the prayer direction into a written prayer
- Length. This is for a person with 20 minutes, not a seminar.

---

Close by returning it where it belongs: this was for you, not for them. Let it stay
that way this week.

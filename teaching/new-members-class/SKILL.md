# New Members Class

Turn attenders into members, and members into people who are actually here.

> Requires: pastor-foundation skill
> **Tier 3.** Drafts fully.

---

## What You Need to Provide

**Required:**
- Format: one Saturday intensive, 3-4 weekly sessions, or a rolling one-on-one

**Helpful:**
- What membership actually means at this church: covenant, vote, baptism requirement,
  a class and a handshake
- Denominational distinctives that must be covered
- What the church believes about baptism and communion, and who may participate
- Governance structure, in plain terms
- Giving expectations, and how directly they get discussed
- What typically goes wrong with the current process
- Typical class size

**Minimum to start:** the format. The rest can be filled in as it surfaces.

Right-size everything to `ATTENDANCE`. A church of 90 does not need a four-week
membership pipeline with a cohort structure.

---

## Step 1: Define the Destination

Before designing sessions, name what someone should be, know, and do at the end.

Most membership classes fail because they are an information dump with no destination.
Someone finishes knowing the church's history and still has no relationships and no
place to serve.

The real goal is not informed members. It is connected ones.

**Output:** three short lists. Know. Believe. Do. Keep each to 3-5 items. If "know" is
longer than the other two combined, the class is a lecture.

---

## Step 2: Session Plan

For each session: title, length, and the arc.

A workable four-part shape, compressed or expanded to fit the format:

1. **Story**: who this church is, where it came from, where it is going. Not a
   timeline. A narrative someone can retell.
2. **Belief**: core doctrine, denominational distinctives, and what is deliberately
   held with an open hand. Naming the open hand builds more trust than pretending
   everything is settled.
3. **Belonging**: how community actually works here: groups, serving, care, and what
   to do when something goes wrong.
4. **Commitment**: what membership means, what is expected, what is offered in
   return, and the practical next step.

Giving belongs in session 4, stated plainly and without apology or pressure. Vague
handling reads as evasive.

---

## Step 3: Teaching Content

For each session:

- **Outline** with time blocks
- **Talking points** in enough detail that someone else could teach it
- **Discussion questions**, because a class that is all talking produces no relationships
- **Scripture** in `BIBLE_TRANSLATION`
- **The question people actually ask here**, and a straight answer

That last item matters. Every session has a predictable hard question: why this church
and not the one down the road, what do you believe about [contested issue], do I have
to give, what if I disagree with something. Answer them directly. Evasion in a
membership class teaches people that hard questions are unwelcome.

---

## Step 4: Participant Handout

Separate from the teaching material, written for the person in the seat:

- What they need to remember, not everything covered
- Beliefs in plain language, no jargon
- Who to contact for what, with actual names and roles
- Space to write
- A clear next step with a date on it

Keep it to two pages. Nobody reads four.

---

## Step 5: The Path After the Class

This is the part most churches skip, and it is where membership either sticks or
evaporates.

- What happens in the week after
- How someone gets into a group, with a specific mechanism, not "we'll connect you"
- How someone starts serving, and who owns making that happen
- The 30-day and 90-day check-in, and who does it
- How you know if it worked

If no one owns the follow-up, say so plainly. A membership class with no path attached
is a ceremony.

---

## Output Format

Markdown to `output/`, two files:

```
output/New-Members-Class-2026-08-02.md          # teaching material
output/New-Members-Handout-2026-08-02.md        # participant handout
```

The handout is written to be printed and should read cleanly on its own.

---

## Quality Bar

**Done looks like:** someone finishes the class knowing what they signed up for,
knowing at least three people's names, and having a specific next step on the calendar.

**Failure modes:**
- Information dump with no relational component
- Dodging giving, governance, or contested doctrine
- No follow-up path, or a path nobody owns
- A handout that duplicates the teaching outline
- Assuming staff capacity the church does not have
